TrainCache v0.9.3 benchmark results

The x-axis is the ratio of dataset bytes to per-node cache size. At rho<=1 the working set fits: TrainCache cuts 1083 s of origin reads to 547 s at 100 Mbit/s, and the prewarmed 300 Mbit/s case is 55 s faster than prewarmed Fluid. Above rho=1 the hit rate falls below 0.32, and the cold 300 Mbit/s, rho=8 case is 35% slower than reading the origin. Origin was measured only at rho=4; the dashed reference is held constant across rho.

Cache fitting (rho=1) and cache short (rho=4) at the same bandwidth. rho=1 is the advantage zone: 49% faster than origin at 100 Mbit/s, and 18% faster than prewarmed Fluid at 300 Mbit/s. rho=4 is the degradation boundary: 9% slower than cold Fluid at 300 Mbit/s, and 5% slower than origin at 100 Mbit/s. The 100 Mbit/s facet has no Fluid or JuiceFS runs.

TrainCache alone as cache size changes. At 100 Mbit/s, rho<=1 takes about 547 s, while rho>=2 rises to 873-1138 s (1062 s at rho=8). At 300 Mbit/s rho<=1 spans 244-330 s and rho>=2 rises to 325-503 s. At 1000 Mbit/s the whole sweep stays at 263-273 s with stall near zero: the bottleneck is compute, not the data path. The dashed line is the origin-only run time at that bandwidth.

The gateway merges duplicate block requests into one origin fetch. Origin only takes 1449 s, local cache only 1321 s, and local cache plus gateway cache 330 s.

Origin egress falls from 50.9 GB to 4.3 GB, a 92% reduction.

At rho=1 the three policies take 76-79 s, within noise. At rho=8 heat priority takes 132 s, LRU 143 s and LFU 148 s. Policy differences only appear when the cache is short.

At rho=1 no policy evicts hot data. At rho=8 heat priority evicts 107 times, LFU 72 and LRU 95. Eviction count is not monotonic with run time: heat priority evicts the most yet finishes fastest, so this metric cannot stand alone as a policy criterion.

Under mixed access the three policies take 69-79 s at rho=2 and 109-116 s at rho=8. The spread is smaller than under a single access pattern.

At rho=8 heat priority evicts hot data 107 times, LFU 72 and LRU 95. At rho<=4 none of the policies evicts hot data to speak of.

The y-axis is the share still resident when the window closes. Both data types stay below 0.12, and from rho=4 they approach zero. The three policies are indistinguishable here; policy choice should rest on run time and hot-data evictions.

No policy triggers forced cleanup on the normal path; every point is zero. The figure confirms eviction never falls back to the cleanup path.

Lookahead 8 is best: 273 s at rho=8. Lookahead 32 is worst: 337-359 s. Raising the replica count from 1 to 3 brings no gain; at rho=8, three replicas are slower (323 s versus 294 s).

At rho=1 with a 60 s wait limit, gating cuts startup from 32.8 s to 3.4 s. A 10 s limit only reaches 29.3 s because the window is too short. At rho=8 gating is 0.3-2.0 s slower than immediate release: with a short cache, gating is pure overhead.

At rho=1 all four order changes settle in 2.3 s. At rho=8 they rise to 6.5-11.4 s, with the steep curriculum slowest at 11.4 s. A short cache amplifies the cost of switching.

When two jobs share a cache the hit rate is 0.39-0.42. Same-node and cross-node placement differ by 0.01-0.03, so sharing a cache brings no clear hit-rate advantage.

Same-node placement takes 408-412 s, cross-node 413-418 s, a 1-3% difference.

The three recovery scenarios average 271 s end to end, with two rounds per cell.

Interruption to resumed training averages 17.5 s: 13.1-13.2 s for checkpoint resume and 22.0-24.9 s when training and agentd restart together. Read errors in the recovery window are zero.

Without a per-connection cap, 92.3 s versus 92.4 s: no difference. Under a 32 Mbit/s per-connection cap, 286 s falls to 107 s. At 16 Mbit/s, 564 s falls to 131 s. With 200 ms RTT and 1% loss, 1135 s falls to 289 s.

Run-time ratios are 2.7x, 4.3x and 3.9x; uncapped is 1.0. The gain comes from spreading a per-connection cap and long RTT over four connections.
